<div style="border-top:4px solid #1d4ed8; margin-top:2em; padding-top:10px;">
<div style="font-size:1.5em; font-weight:bold; color:#1d4ed8;">Decode the names</div>
<div>Most names have two parts: <b>what acts on the material</b> + <b>what changes in it</b>.</div>
</div>

{{2}}
**First part: the cause**

{{2}}
| Prefix | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Piezo- | pressure, mechanical force (Greek "to press") |
| Pyro- | heat (Greek "fire") |
| Thermo- | temperature |
| Magneto- | magnetic field |
| Photo- | light |

{{2}}
**Second part: the effect**

{{2}}
| Ending | Meaning |
|---|---|
| -electric | an electric charge or voltage appears |
| -resistive | the electrical resistance changes |
| -elastic | mechanical stress or deformation is involved |
| -optic | the optical behavior changes |
| -magnetic | the magnetic properties change |

{{3}}
<div style="border-left:6px solid #d97706; background:#fef3c7; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#b45309;">Try it</div>
<b>Piezo</b> + <b>resistive</b> = a force changes the electrical resistance.<br>
<b>Photo</b> + <b>electric</b> = light produces an electrical signal.<br>
<b>Thermo</b> + <b>optic</b> = temperature changes how light passes through the material.
</div>

{{3}}
<div style="border-left:6px solid #0284c7; background:#e0f2fe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#0369a1;">Good to know</div>
<b>Eigenfrequency</b> is the odd one out: "Eigen" is German for "own". It is the frequency at which a system oscillates by itself, and it shifts when mass, geometry or elasticity change.<br>
<b>Thermoelectric</b> effects work in both directions: a temperature difference gives a voltage (Seebeck), and a current can heat or cool a junction (Peltier).
</div>

{{4}}
<div style="border-top:4px solid #1d4ed8; margin-top:2em; padding-top:10px;">
<div style="font-size:1.5em; font-weight:bold; color:#1d4ed8;">Cheat sheet</div>
<div>All 13 at a glance. The next slides explain the first six in detail.</div>
</div>

{{4}}
| Phenomenon | Cause → Effect |
|---|---|
| Piezoelectric | mechanical force → electric charge |
| Pyroelectric | change in temperature → electric charge |
| Piezoresistive | mechanical stress → change in electrical resistance |
| Thermoelectric | temperature difference ↔ electric voltage (Seebeck, Peltier, Thomson) |
| Thermoresistive | temperature → change in electrical resistance |
| Eigenfrequency | change in mass, shape or elasticity → shift of the natural frequency |
| Magnetoresistive | magnetic field → change in electrical resistance |
| Photoelectric | light → electrical signal |
| Magnetoelastic | magnetic field ↔ mechanical stress |
| Photoelastic | mechanical stress → change in optical behavior |
| Photomagnetic | light → change in magnetic properties |
| Thermoelastic | temperature → mechanical deformation |
| Thermooptic | temperature → change in optical properties |

## <span style="color: blue">Piezoelectric Phenomenon</span>
<div style="position: absolute; top: 10px; right: 10px; z-index: 1000;">
  <img src="../Images/Logo.uni.png" alt="Logo" style="width:80px;">
</div>

<div style="display:flex; flex-wrap:wrap; gap:10px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#60a5fa,#2563eb); color:#fff; border-radius:20px; padding:6px 18px; font-weight:bold;">Discovered 1880 – 1881</div>
<div style="background:#dbeafe; color:#1e3a8a; border:2px solid #2563eb; border-radius:20px; padding:6px 18px;">by the French physicists Jacques and Pierre Curie</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #dc2626; background:#fee2e2; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#b91c1c;">The essence</div>
When a <b>mechanical force</b> is applied to a crystal, the crystal becomes <b>electrically polarized</b>.
</div>

<div style="text-align:center;">
<video src="../Images/movig.quartz.mov" autoplay muted loop playsinline style="width:400px; max-width:100%;"></video>
</div>

[source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezoelectric_sensor)

<div style="display:flex; flex-wrap:wrap; gap:14px; margin:1.2em 0;">
<div style="flex:1; min-width:240px; border-top:6px solid #2563eb; background:#dbeafe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:10px 16px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#1d4ed8;">Compression</div>
Squeezing the crystal produces electric charges on its surfaces.
</div>
<div style="flex:1; min-width:240px; border-top:6px solid #7c3aed; background:#ede9fe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:10px 16px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#6d28d9;">Stretching</div>
Pulling the crystal produces charges of the <b>opposite polarity</b>.
</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #d97706; background:#fef3c7; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
The size of the polarization is <b>proportional</b> to the size of the mechanical action: twice the force gives twice the charge.
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #0284c7; background:#e0f2fe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#0369a1;">Why does it happen?</div>
In the unloaded crystal, the centers of the positive and the negative charges sit at the same point, so the crystal looks neutral from outside. A force shifts the ions, the two centers move apart, and charges of opposite sign appear on opposite faces of the crystal.
</div>

![quarz.bluered](../Images/quarz.bluered.png "<small><a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/s21041112?urlappend=%3Futm_source%3Dresearchgate.net%26utm_medium%3Darticle">illustrating the piezoelectric effect</a></small>")


<div style="text-align:center;">
<video src="../Images/effect.p.mov" autoplay muted loop playsinline style="width:400px; max-width:100%;"></video>
</div>

[The Piezoelectric Effect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y2pAFuSciB4)
 


<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#1d4ed8; margin-top:1.5em;">The effect works in both directions</div>

<div style="display:flex; flex-wrap:wrap; gap:14px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="flex:1; min-width:240px; border-top:6px solid #2563eb; background:#dbeafe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:10px 16px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#1d4ed8;">Direct effect</div>
A mechanical force produces an electric charge.
</div>
<div style="flex:1; min-width:240px; border-top:6px solid #ea580c; background:#ffedd5; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:10px 16px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#c2410c;">Reverse effect</div>
An electric field makes the crystal change its shape.
</div>
</div>

The next slides look at both effects in detail.

<div style="border-left:6px solid #16a34a; background:#dcfce7; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#15803d;">In simple words</div>
"Piezo" comes from the Greek word for "to press". Press a piezoelectric crystal and it produces electricity. This is how many gas lighters make their spark: a small hammer hits a crystal, and the charge that appears creates the spark.
</div>

**Quick check:** Which statement describes the direct piezoelectric effect?

- [(X)] A force on the crystal produces an electric charge
- [( )] A voltage on the crystal makes it change its shape
- [( )] Light on the crystal produces an electric current

<div style="border-top:4px solid #1d4ed8; margin-top:2em; padding-top:10px;">
<div style="font-size:1.5em; font-weight:bold; color:#1d4ed8;">Piezoelectric material</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #2563eb; background:#dbeafe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
Piezoelectrics are characterized by a material’s crystal structure with a <b>covalent or ionic bond without a center of symmetry</b>.
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #7c3aed; background:#ede9fe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
Materials with <b>low conductivity</b>, in which there are negligibly few free charge carriers, are characterized by <b>high piezoelectric performance</b>.
</div>

![Tourma](../Images/Tourma.png "<small><a href="https://sijr.sahyadri.edu.in/Articles/Volume%203/Issue%201/6.pdf">Crystal structure of a piezoelectric</a></small>")


<div style="border-left:6px solid #0d9488; background:#ccfbf1; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
Many piezoelectrics are made on the basis of substances with the formula <b>ABO<sub>3</sub></b>, such as BaTiO<sub>3</sub>, PbTiO<sub>3</sub>. These materials have a polycrystalline (consisting of many crystals) structure.
</div>

<div style="display:flex; align-items:center; gap:14px; margin:1em 0 1em 24px;">
<div style="flex-shrink:0; width:60px; height:30px; background:#2563eb; clip-path:polygon(0 28%,62% 28%,62% 0,100% 50%,62% 100%,62% 72%,0 72%);"></div>
<div style="font-size:1.5em; font-weight:bold; font-style:italic; color:#1d4ed8;">Ferroelectrics!</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #db2777; background:#fce7f3; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#be185d;">What are ferroelectrics?</div>
Crystals that show the phenomenon of <b>ferroelectricity</b> are called ferroelectrics. In ferroelectricity, a <b>spontaneous polarization</b> occurs in the crystal within a certain temperature range, even without an external electric field. This polarization can be reoriented by applying such a field.<br>
In the picture above, (b) shows the structure below the critical temperature T<sub>c</sub> with the spontaneous polarization P, and (a) shows it above T<sub>c</sub>.
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #ea580c; background:#ffedd5; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
<b><i>Piezoelectric semiconductors:</i></b> Cadmium sulfide CdS, zinc oxide ZnO, zinc sulfide ZnS, cadmium selenide CdSe, gallium arsenide GaAs, etc. are examples of semiconductor-type compounds with ionic-covalent bonding.
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #16a34a; background:#dcfce7; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#15803d;">In simple words</div>
<b>Why no center of symmetry?</b> In a crystal with a center of symmetry, a force moves the positive and negative charges in a balanced way, so no net charge appears. Without that symmetry, the charge centers shift differently and the crystal becomes polarized.<br>
<b>Why low conductivity?</b> Free charge carriers can move around and cancel the charges that the force creates. With very few of them, the charges stay and can be used.
</div>

**Quick check:** Which crystal property is needed for a material to be piezoelectric?

- [(X)] A crystal structure without a center of symmetry
- [( )] Very high electrical conductivity
- [( )] A large number of free charge carriers


## <span style="color: blue">Direct and Reverse Effect</span>

<div style="display:inline-block; background:linear-gradient(135deg,#60a5fa,#1d4ed8); color:#fff; border-radius:14px; padding:14px 36px; font-size:1.4em; font-weight:bold; box-shadow:5px 5px 0 #1e3a8a;">Piezoelectric Phenomenon</div>

<div style="border-top:4px solid #2563eb; margin-top:2em; padding-top:10px;">
<div style="font-size:1.5em; font-weight:bold; color:#1d4ed8;">Direct piezoelectric effect</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #2563eb; background:#dbeafe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
The <b>direct piezoelectric effect</b> is characterized by the <b>electric polarization of the dielectric</b>, which occurs due to the action of an <b>external mechanical stress</b> on it, while the charge induced on the surface of the dielectric is <b>proportional to the applied mechanical stress</b>.
</div>

<div style="border:4px solid #7c3aed; border-radius:20px; padding:10px 14px 16px 14px; margin:1.2em 0; text-align:center;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-style:italic; color:#6d28d9; margin-bottom:10px;">Direct piezoelectric effect</div>
<div style="width:100%; white-space:nowrap; font-size:0;">
<div style="display:inline-block; vertical-align:middle; width:38%; box-sizing:border-box; white-space:normal; font-size:clamp(12px,2vw,16px); border:3px solid #2563eb; color:#1f2937; padding:10px 4px;">1<br>Mechanical force</div>
<div style="display:inline-block; vertical-align:middle; width:14%;"><div style="height:26px; width:70%; margin:0 auto; background:#f59e0b; clip-path:polygon(0 28%,62% 28%,62% 0,100% 50%,62% 100%,62% 72%,0 72%);"></div></div>
<div style="display:inline-block; vertical-align:middle; width:38%; box-sizing:border-box; white-space:normal; font-size:clamp(12px,2vw,16px); border:3px solid #dc2626; color:#b91c1c; font-weight:bold; padding:10px 4px;">2<br>Polarization of solids</div>
</div>
</div>

![pdt](../Images/pdt.png "<small><a href="https://doi.org/10.5545/sv-jme.2016.3656">Direct piezoelectric effect</a></small>")


<div style="border-left:6px solid #7c3aed; background:#f5f3ff; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
The dependence of <b>polarization (P)</b> on mechanical stress is <b>linear</b> and is described by the expression:
<div style="text-align:center; font-size:2em; font-weight:bold; margin:10px 0;"><span style="color:#7c3aed;"><i>P</i></span> = <span style="color:#ea580c;"><i>d</i></span> · <span style="color:#2563eb;"><i>T</i></span></div>
<div style="display:flex; flex-wrap:wrap; justify-content:center; gap:16px; font-size:0.95em;">
<div><b style="color:#7c3aed;"><i>P</i></b> = polarization</div>
<div><b style="color:#ea580c;"><i>d</i></b> = coefficient: piezoelectric modulus</div>
<div><b style="color:#2563eb;"><i>T</i></b> = mechanical stress</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="border-top:4px solid #ea580c; margin-top:2em; padding-top:10px;">
<div style="font-size:1.5em; font-weight:bold; color:#c2410c;">Reverse piezoelectric effect</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #ea580c; background:#ffedd5; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
With the <b>reverse piezoelectric effect</b>, the phenomenon manifests itself in the opposite way: the dielectric <b>changes its dimensions</b> under the action of an <b>external electric field</b> applied to it, while the magnitude of the mechanical deformation (relative deformation) is <b>proportional to the strength of the electric field</b> applied to the sample.
</div>

![rde](../Images/rde.png "<small><a href="https://www.semiconductorforu.com/what-is-piezoelectric-transducer/">Reverse piezoelectric effect</a></small>")

<div style="border-left:6px solid #16a34a; background:#f0fdf4; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
When the <b>direction</b> of the applied electric field changes, the <b>direction of deformation</b> changes. Here the dependence is also <b>linear</b>:
<div style="text-align:center; font-size:2em; font-weight:bold; margin:10px 0;"><span style="color:#16a34a;"><i>r</i></span> = <span style="color:#ea580c;"><i>d</i></span> · <span style="color:#dc2626;"><i>E</i></span></div>
<div style="display:flex; flex-wrap:wrap; justify-content:center; gap:16px; font-size:0.95em;">
<div><b style="color:#16a34a;"><i>r</i></b> = deformation</div>
<div><b style="color:#ea580c;"><i>d</i></b> = coefficient: piezoelectric modulus</div>
<div><b style="color:#dc2626;"><i>E</i></b> = electric field strength</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #ea580c; background:#fff7ed; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#c2410c;">One coefficient for both directions</div>
The <b>coefficient <i>d</i></b> is the <b>same for the direct and the reverse piezoelectric effect</b> for all substances.
</div>

<div style="border-top:4px solid #1d4ed8; margin-top:2em; padding-top:10px;">
<div style="font-size:1.5em; font-weight:bold; color:#1d4ed8;">Side by side</div>
</div>

| | Direct effect | Reverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | mechanical force (stress) | electric field |
| Effect | electric polarization, charge on the surface | change of dimensions (deformation) |
| Formula | P = d · T | r = d · E |
| Coefficient | d | the same d |

<div style="border-left:6px solid #0284c7; background:#e0f2fe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#0369a1;">In simple words</div>
<b>Direct:</b> squeeze the crystal and you get an electrical signal. That is what a <b>sensor</b> does (see Lecture 1).<br>
<b>Reverse:</b> apply an electrical signal and the crystal changes its shape, a small movement. That is what an <b>actuator</b> does.
</div>

## <span style="color: blue">Piezoelectric Applications</span>

<div style="position:absolute; top:10px; right:10px; z-index:1000;">
<img src="../Images/Logo.uni.png" alt="Logo" style="width:80px;">
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #dc2626; background:#fee2e2; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.3em; color:#b91c1c;">The most famous application: an ignition element</div>
<div style="margin-top:6px;">The piezoelectric effect is used in <b>pocket lighters</b> or <b>kitchen igniters for gas stoves</b>. When you press the crystal, a <b>potential difference</b> arises and a <b>spark</b> appears in the air gap.</div>
</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #0d9488; background:#ccfbf1; color:#1f2937; border-radius:10px; padding:12px 18px; margin:1.2em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; font-size:1.2em; color:#0f766e;">Electronic stethoscope</div>
<div style="margin-top:6px;">Video: <a href="https://www.bing.com/videos/search?q=understanding+piezoelectric+effect" target="_blank">Understanding Piezoelectric effect!</a></div>
</div>

<div style="display:flex; flex-wrap:wrap; gap:16px; margin:1.5em 0;">

<div style="text-align:center;">
<video src="../Images/apps.p.mov" autoplay muted loop playsinline style="width:400px; max-width:100%;"></video>
</div>

<div style="flex:1 1 300px;">
<div style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#60a5fa,#2563eb); color:#fff; border-radius:12px 12px 0 0; padding:10px 16px; text-align:center;"><b style="font-size:1.2em;">Direct effect</b><br><small>a force produces an electrical signal</small></div>
<div style="border-left:6px solid #2563eb; background:#dbeafe; color:#1f2937; padding:10px 16px; margin-top:10px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#1d4ed8;">Strain sensors</div>
Crystals with this effect can be used as <b>strain sensors</b>, but this scope of use is limited by the property of the piezoelectric effect to appear <b>only in dynamics</b>: if the changes stop, the signal stops generating.
</div>
<div style="border-left:6px solid #2563eb; background:#dbeafe; color:#1f2937; padding:10px 16px; margin-top:10px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#1d4ed8;">Microphone</div>
Piezo crystals can be used <b>as a microphone</b>: when exposed to acoustic waves, electrical signals are formed.
</div>
<div style="border:3px solid #7c3aed; background:#ede9fe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin-top:10px;">
<b style="color:#6d28d9;">Can also be used in</b> <b><i>pressure, deformation, vibration, acceleration sensors</i></b>
</div>
</div>

<div style="flex:1 1 300px;">
<div style="background:linear-gradient(135deg,#fb923c,#ea580c); color:#fff; border-radius:12px 12px 0 0; padding:10px 16px; text-align:center;"><b style="font-size:1.2em;">Reverse effect</b><br><small>an electrical signal produces sound</small></div>
<div style="border-left:6px solid #ea580c; background:#ffedd5; color:#1f2937; padding:10px 16px; margin-top:10px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#c2410c;">Sound emitters</div>
The reverse piezoelectric effect also allows (sometimes simultaneously) the use of such elements as <b>sound emitters</b>. When an electrical signal is applied to the crystal, the element begins to generate acoustic waves. Such emitters are widely used to create <b>ultrasonic waves</b>, in particular in <b>medical technology</b>.
</div>
<div style="border-left:6px solid #ea580c; background:#ffedd5; color:#1f2937; padding:10px 16px; margin-top:10px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#c2410c;">Acoustic filter</div>
In this case, the resonant properties of the plate can be used as an <b>acoustic filter</b> that selects only natural frequency waves.
</div>
<div style="border-left:6px solid #ea580c; background:#ffedd5; color:#1f2937; padding:10px 16px; margin-top:10px;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#c2410c;">Sound generator</div>
A piezoelectric element can be used <b>in a sound generator</b> (siren, detector, etc.) simultaneously as a <b>frequency-setting and sound-emitting element</b>. The sound is always generated at the <b>resonant frequency</b>, and you can get the maximum volume with little energy.
</div>
</div>

</div>

<div style="border-left:6px solid #0284c7; background:#e0f2fe; color:#1f2937; border-radius:8px; padding:10px 16px; margin:1em 0;">
<div style="font-weight:bold; color:#0369a1;">In simple words</div>
Everything on the left side <b>measures</b> something: a force goes in, an electrical signal comes out. Everything on the right side <b>produces</b> something: an electrical signal goes in, and sound comes out. The "resonant" and "natural" frequency on the right is the topic of the Eigenfrequency phenomenon later in this lecture.
</div>
